The hallway smelled faintly of rain, though no window had been opened for years. Every door wore the same brass number, polished bright as if someone still expected visitors.

Mara walked slowly, counting her steps because the old caretaker had told her not to lose track. At fifty steps, she should see the stairwell. At seventy, she should turn back.

At sixty-nine, the lights warmed from white to amber, and a gramophone began playing somewhere behind the walls. The tune was soft and patient, the kind of music her grandmother used to hum when storms crossed the valley.

Mara stopped. The nearest door clicked once.

On its brass plate, the number changed from 14 to her own apartment number.
